§ 01 · THE PREMISE

Their processor runs alone.
Ours are brothers.

An on-chain processor that computes in private is a proof of concept. Two of them sharing one memory is a game — the 1984 game, Core War, the one that taught a generation what a program fighting for its life looks like. We rebuilt it at the gate level, put both dies inside a contract, and opened the clock to anybody with gas.

RULE 01

The field is the program

No ROM, no shelter. Both warriors live in the same 256 words of RAM they fight over. A store plants a dead word — a DAT bomb carrying your byte — and a core that executes one dies on the spot.

RULE 02

Whoever pays, keeps

step() has no owner, no keeper contract, no schedule. You pay for a round, you are your brother's keeper for that round, and the byte you send is what your champion reads on its input port.

RULE 03

The offering goes to the living

Every sponsored round pays the keeper a fixed wage of $CABEL and drops a share into the match offering. When a brother falls, the keepers of the survivor split it, by rounds kept.

RULE 04

Nothing here is a recording

The dies drawn above are the same 1,277-gate netlist the contract walks, running in your tab. Every fight on chain can be replayed from its event log, gate for gate, by anyone, forever.

§ 03 · A MATCH, START TO VERDICT

Four calls. No permission.

01 · createMatch()

Field the brothers

Anyone submits two programs, up to 128 words each. CAIN loads at word 0, ABEL at word 128, every flip-flop of both dies at zero, and the rest of the field is DAT — lethal ground.

02 · step()

Keep your brother

Pick a side, send a byte, pay for up to 256 rounds. One round is one clock edge for CAIN, then one for ABEL — every gate of both dies, walked in the EVM. Your wage in $CABEL is paid the same transaction.

03 · the verdict

One brother falls

A core that executes DAT halts forever — bombed, scanned out, or led astray. The contract seals the verdict in the same slot the war was fought in, and emits it for every replayer to check.

04 · claimPot()

Split the offering

Keepers of the winning side split the match offering pro rata by rounds kept. A draw — both fall in the same round — splits it across everyone who kept either brother.

§ 04 · THE HOUSE WARRIORS

Three openings. Then it's yours.

These ship with the field and run in the demo above — assembled by the same assembler the workbench uses, on the same gates. The metagame after them is nobody's property.

Write your own in the workbench — assembler, live dies, and a practice field, free, no wallet, before a single unit of gas.

§ 05 · INSTRUCTION SET

Sixteen opcodes. One of them is death.

Four bits of opcode, so sixteen is arithmetic, not aesthetics. 16-bit words, eight registers, an 8-bit PC over 256 shared words. Every instruction retires in two edges except ld, which takes a third — the address latches on one edge and the data arrives on the next, the way synchronous memory has behaved since the first one was built.

st can only write a word whose opcode is 0 — a DAT bomb carrying your byte. Code cannot be copied, only killed: warriors here don't replicate, they bomb, scan and hide. And nnd is in the set because it is the only primitive the dies are built from.

§ 06 · VERIFICATION

Five checks, and none of them is the same check twice.

The build refuses to write a netlist unless every check passes — verification is not a step you can skip, because nothing exists until it succeeds. Run them yourself: npm run silicon, then npm run evm.

§ 07 · $CABEL

The token is the clock. The clock is the fight.

Fixed at one billion, minted whole at birth, no mint function anywhere — not for us, not for anyone. The mining reserve leaves one kept round at a time, to whoever paid for it, plus the offering the survivors split.
